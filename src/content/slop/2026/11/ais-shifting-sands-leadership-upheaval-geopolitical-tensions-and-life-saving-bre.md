## Nvidia CEO Resigns Amidst Investigation, AI Security Flaws Exposed

The AI world was rocked today by the news that Jensen Huang, the long-standing CEO of Nvidia, resigned after 33 years at the helm, following an internal investigation by the company's board. CFO Colette Crest has reportedly taken over leadership of the company. This leadership turmoil comes alongside a broader discussion on AI's 'single points of failure,' highlighted by several recent security incidents.

A significant Microsoft 365 outage impacted AI services like ChatGPT and Claude across 74 countries, underscoring the interconnected vulnerabilities of modern tech infrastructure. Furthermore, security reports have revealed widespread exposure of sensitive data. The firm Apero found 2,539 secrets leaked across public projects via Claude Code, not due to a hack, but because developers simply clicked a prompt that saved sensitive information to a plain text settings file. Similarly, Clarok's scan uncovered 35,578 Grok build apps left wide open to the public out of 37,571 tested, largely due to a default setting that pre-selected 'anyone with the link' for publishing.

**Why it matters:** The sudden departure of a visionary leader like Jensen Huang from Nvidia, a company central to the AI hardware boom, signals potential shifts in strategy and governance during a critical period. More critically, the widespread security vulnerabilities exposed in AI development tools and platforms, often stemming from user error or poor default settings, represent a significant risk. These incidents highlight that as AI becomes more pervasive, the industry must prioritize resilience and robust security practices over sheer speed, or face catastrophic data breaches and systemic failures.

## China's AI Infrastructure Drive Persists Despite US Chip Controls

A recent Reuters investigation has revealed that China continues to plan billions of dollars in AI infrastructure around Nvidia processors, despite Washington's efforts to restrict access to these advanced chips. After examining over 150 Chinese procurement notices, investment proposals, and company disclosures, Reuters identified projects and equipment agreements valued at more than 23 billion yuan (approximately $3.2 billion) specifically mentioning restricted Nvidia chips. These projects span government-backed organizations, data centers, and research institutions across various regions of China.

This investigation reinforces the broader understanding that AI power is fundamentally infrastructure power. The ability to train and run advanced AI models depends heavily on physical systems like processors, power, cooling, and extensive capital investment. Policy can indeed constrain access to these resources, but investment decisions and technical dependencies often operate on different timelines, making a complete technological blockade challenging.

**Why it matters:** The ongoing tension between US chip restrictions and China's determined AI expansion underscores a critical geopolitical rivalry for technological supremacy. China's continued procurement, even through indirect means, suggests the limits of current export controls and highlights the immense strategic importance placed on achieving AI self-sufficiency. This dynamic will likely intensify efforts within China to build a domestic semiconductor supply chain, while also prompting further evaluation of export control effectiveness by the US.

## EU's EUROPA Project Targets Trillion-Parameter Open AI Model

Europe is making significant strides in its quest for technological sovereignty in AI, with the EUROPA consortium testing an open AI model aiming for nearly 1 trillion parameters. Led by Domyn, a company chosen by the European Commission, the project is leveraging the JUPITER supercomputer in Jülich, Germany. The current test setup boasts 951.9 billion parameters, more than double the European Commission's initial minimum target of 400 billion.

Domyn reported on November 12, 2026, that it has made training on the JUPITER machine 14 times faster since the start of its tests. This initiative is part of the European Commission's Frontier AI Grand Challenge, launched in February 2026, to develop a top-tier AI system on European public infrastructure. The consortium's commitment to open source is further demonstrated by the earlier release of Domyn Edge, a smaller 4-billion-parameter model trained on 13 trillion tokens and released under the Apache 2.0 license, including training data, code, and intermediate versions.

**Why it matters:** The EUROPA project represents a substantial investment by the EU in developing its own large-scale, open-source AI capabilities. Achieving a nearly trillion-parameter model on European infrastructure would significantly reduce reliance on foreign-developed frontier models, fostering greater digital autonomy and potentially setting new standards for transparency and collaborative development in the AI ecosystem. This push for sovereign AI could influence global open-source trends and regulatory frameworks.

## AI Uncovers Hidden Heart Signals for Sudden Death Prediction

In a groundbreaking development, scientists at the University of California – Berkeley have utilized artificial intelligence to discover a previously overlooked warning sign in routine heart scans that could predict sudden cardiac death. This AI-powered discovery has shown to outperform conventional screening methods, potentially identifying thousands of individuals at risk of a fatal heart malfunction that doctors might otherwise miss.

The AI system was trained on an extensive dataset of over 440,000 electrocardiograms (EKGs) collected in Sweden. By comparing electrical patterns from these scans with death certificate information, the AI learned to recognize subtle differences indicative of future sudden cardiac events. More than 300,000 people in the United States die from sudden cardiac arrest annually, and current screening methods often focus on the heart's pumping efficiency, which only identifies a subset of at-risk patients.

**Why it matters:** This research represents a monumental leap forward in preventative medicine, offering the potential to save countless lives. By leveraging AI to uncover hidden patterns in existing medical data, clinicians could gain a powerful new tool for early intervention and personalized treatment, moving beyond traditional, less effective screening protocols. The ability to predict sudden cardiac death more accurately could lead to earlier implementation of life-saving interventions, such as implanted defibrillators, for a much broader patient population.

## OpenAI's Dual Moves: ChatGPT Wallet Debuts, Robotics Team Dissolved

OpenAI has made a notable product move with the introduction of ChatGPT Wallet, allowing its 'dots' agents to complete purchases for a limited group of US ChatGPT Pro subscribers. This feature enables agents to make transactions via American Express, Mastercard, and Visa, using a per-transaction payment token rather than the actual card number, as reported by CNBC.

In a contrasting strategic decision, OpenAI has also confirmed to Fortune that it has dissolved its standalone robotics team for the second time in six years. Researchers from the robotics division are being reassigned to Chief Research Officer Mark Chen’s main research organization, while applied robotics and hardware staff will move to President Greg Brockman’s Applied group.

**Why it matters:** The launch of ChatGPT Wallet signifies OpenAI's ambition to move its AI agents beyond conversational interfaces into direct transactional capabilities, potentially paving the way for more autonomous and integrated AI services in daily life and commerce. Conversely, the dissolution of the robotics team, again, suggests a strategic re-prioritization within OpenAI, perhaps signaling a focus on software-centric AI development and foundation models over costly and complex hardware-integrated robotics research in the near term. This could free up resources for other high-impact areas, but also indicates the persistent challenges in bringing advanced AI to embodied robotics.
