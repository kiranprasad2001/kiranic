## Frontier Models Slash Prices and Boost Performance in Intense AI Race

The past 24 hours have seen a significant intensification of the AI model race, with leading developers OpenAI and Anthropic simultaneously releasing new, more efficient models that offer substantial cost reductions and performance gains. OpenAI launched **GPT-6 Sol** and **GPT-6 Luna** on September 22, pricing them at half the cost of their GPT-5.6 series counterparts ($2 per million input tokens for Sol vs. $4 previously), while claiming Sol significantly reduces errors in complex coding tasks, achieving 'Astra-level reliability'.

Hot on OpenAI's heels, Anthropic released **Claude Opus 5.5**, also on September 22, with a listed price of $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output, representing a 20% reduction from Opus 5. Anthropic further noted a 60% drop in cache-read costs to $0.20 per million, which they claim translates to approximately 40% overall savings on typical agentic and coding workloads. Opus 5.5 also demonstrated marked performance improvements on benchmarks like Terminal-Bench 4.0 and OSWorld 2.0. This rapid-fire release strategy, offering advanced capabilities at significantly lower price points, signals a maturing market where efficiency and cost-effectiveness are becoming critical differentiators alongside raw intelligence.

**Why it matters:** This aggressive pricing and performance competition is a huge win for developers and enterprises. Lower token costs and improved model reliability mean that sophisticated AI applications, especially those involving complex agentic workflows and extensive coding, become more economically viable and performant. This accelerates the deployment of AI in real-world scenarios and puts pressure on all model providers to innovate on both capability and efficiency.
