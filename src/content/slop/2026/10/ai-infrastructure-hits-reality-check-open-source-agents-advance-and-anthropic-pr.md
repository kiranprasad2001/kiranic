## AI Infrastructure Build-Out Stalls Amid Power Grid Delays and Funding Woes

The ambitious expansion of AI computing infrastructure is running into significant real-world obstacles, with numerous data center projects across the globe facing delays, withdrawals, or outright blocks. A Financial Times investigation revealed that at least 47 major U.S. data center projects have been stalled in 2026, largely due to challenges in securing adequate power grid connections and escalating financing costs. Bernstein analysts estimate that only about 35% of the planned U.S. pipeline is credible, underscoring the severity of the bottleneck.

This infrastructure crunch is already impacting major players. Microsoft, for instance, has withdrawn from an agreement to lease computing capacity at a planned AI data center in Loughton, UK, operated by Nscale. The decision stemmed from reported delays by UK Power Networks, which informed Nscale that sufficient power would not be available until the early to mid-2030s, years beyond the original operational target. Concurrently, concerns are rising over the financial sustainability of AI infrastructure spending, with reports of CoreWeave's borrowing costs jumping significantly, testing investors' willingness to finance the massive capital requirements.

**Why it matters:** The AI industry's exponential growth is heavily reliant on underlying compute infrastructure. These widespread delays and funding challenges signal a critical bottleneck that could slow down AI development and deployment across the board, impacting everything from model training to enterprise adoption. It highlights the physical realities – and limitations – of the digital AI boom.

## Tenstorrent Secures $1.5 Billion to Scale RISC-V AI Chips

In a significant boost to the AI hardware landscape, Tenstorrent, an AI chip startup, has raised $1.5 billion in a late-stage funding round, more than doubling its valuation to $6.2 billion. The Series E round was led by Trace Capital and Innovation X, with participation from notable investors including Cathie Wood's ARK Invest and T. Rowe Price.

The Santa Clara, California-based company plans to leverage this capital to scale the production of its Galaxy servers, which integrate dozens of its AI chips to power demanding applications like chatbots, coding assistants, and video generators. A key differentiator for Tenstorrent is its focus on RISC-V, an open-source chip architecture. This strategic choice allows companies to design processors without incurring license fees, thereby reducing dependence on proprietary chip ecosystems such as Arm's.

**Why it matters:** This substantial investment in Tenstorrent not only validates the market's confidence in specialized AI hardware but also highlights the growing importance of the open-source RISC-V architecture. As AI workloads become more diverse and demanding, open alternatives to dominant proprietary solutions could foster greater innovation, reduce costs, and democratize access to high-performance AI compute.

## Anthropic Proposes AI Safety Framework with Clear Intervention Thresholds and Public Benefit Fund

Anthropic, a leading AI research lab, is taking a proactive stance on AI regulation by pitching its own AI safety bill to the U.S. Congress. The proposed legislation includes specific thresholds for government intervention in cases of extreme AI risk: an imminent risk of over $1 billion in damage or more than 50 people killed or seriously injured, stemming from threats like bioweapons, cyberattacks, or a model evading control. A federal judge would be responsible for deciding on such interventions, which Anthropic describes as "sensible policies."

Further demonstrating its commitment to responsible AI development, Anthropic is also committing 5% of its equity to a new Public Benefit Fund LLC. An additional 2% will be added at the board's discretion for every 50% rise in company value above a set baseline, as the company reportedly heads towards a November IPO.

**Why it matters:** Anthropic's move to propose concrete, quantifiable thresholds for AI safety intervention marks a significant development in the ongoing AI regulation debate. While the specific figures may be contentious, it represents a major AI lab actively engaging with policymakers to define the boundaries of responsible AI. The establishment of a Public Benefit Fund also signals a long-term commitment to aligning corporate success with broader societal well-being in the age of advanced AI.

## Open-Weight Mixture-of-Experts Models and Agent Orchestration Gain Momentum

The landscape of AI engineering is undergoing a notable transformation, with a decisive shift towards autonomous agent orchestration and the widespread release of powerful open-weight Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models. This week, the center of gravity in AI engineering moved towards labs giving models direct authority to design their own multiagent systems, rather than developers manually scripting every interaction.

This trend is bolstered by the emergence of massive open-weight MoE models, with some crossing the 800 billion parameter mark. Notable releases include DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017, which has made its weights open source under the MIT license, and Reflection AI's Beam, a 501 billion parameter Apache 2.0 MoE model that boasts efficiency with comparable reasoning to larger models at significantly less inference compute. These developments reinforce that advanced agentic coding and complex reasoning capabilities are increasingly accessible outside of closed API ecosystems.

**Why it matters:** The rise of self-organizing agents and powerful open-weight MoE models represents a democratizing force in AI development. By enabling models to orchestrate complex tasks and providing highly capable models with open licenses, these advancements empower a broader range of developers and researchers to build sophisticated AI applications, pushing the boundaries of what's possible in an increasingly decentralized manner. This could accelerate innovation and foster new use cases for AI agents.

## The Bottom Line

Today's AI signals highlight a critical juncture for the industry: the foundational infrastructure supporting the AI boom is facing serious practical and financial hurdles, even as innovation in model architectures and autonomous agents continues at a rapid pace. The convergence of proactive regulatory engagement from major players like Anthropic and the increasing power of open-source models suggests a future where AI's technical advancements must be carefully balanced with robust physical infrastructure and thoughtful governance frameworks.
